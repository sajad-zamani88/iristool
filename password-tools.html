Password Strength Meter

A continuous 0–100 strength score built on entropy (length × log₂ charset), plus the numbers that explain it: charset size, total combinations, and time-to-crack under two attacks — 10 billion guesses per second for a stolen-hash offline attack, 100 per second for a throttled online login. It ends with one concrete suggestion.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
How is password strength calculated?

The core is Shannon-style entropy: length × log₂(charset size), where the charset counts lowercase (26), uppercase (26), digits (10) and symbols (33). The score maps that entropy onto 0–100, and collapses toward zero when the password contains a common pattern like "password", "123456" or a single repeated character — because attackers try those first.

What do the crack-time estimates mean?

They divide the total keyspace (2^entropy) by an attack speed. Ten billion guesses per second models an offline attack on a stolen hash with modern GPUs; one hundred guesses per second models a throttled online login. They are order-of-magnitude estimates, not guarantees — real attackers also use dictionaries and leaked-password lists, which is why common patterns are penalized so hard.

Is my password sent anywhere?

No. The analysis runs entirely inside your browser tab — the page has no backend and makes no requests with your input. Still, as a general habit, never type your real passwords into any website's demo tool, including this one; use the Sample button to play safely.

What is the difference between the checker and the meter?

The Strong Password Checker answers yes/no with an eight-rule checklist — great for validating against a policy. The Password Strength Meter gives a continuous score with entropy, combinations and crack-time estimates — great for understanding why a password is weak and how much longer or weirder it needs to be.
