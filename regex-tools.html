Regex Tester

runs in the bench above

The lab runs on your browser's own ECMAScript RegExp engine — the same one your code will use in production, so what you see is what will ship. Every match is underlined in the test string the moment it exists, listed with its index, and exploded into numbered $1…$n and named <name> capture groups. Flip the g, i, m, s, u flags and the whole picture re-renders. A pattern that stops compiling turns the field red and prints the engine's exact message — no submit button between you and the answer.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
Which regex flavor does the tester use?

JavaScript's native ECMAScript RegExp engine — the same one that runs in every browser. That means full support for named groups (?<name>…), lookbehind (?<=…), Unicode property escapes like \p{L} with the u flag, and dotall with s. PCRE-only constructs like \K or atomic groups aren't part of the ECMAScript spec and won't work here.

Why does my pattern show an error?

The tester surfaces the engine's exact message the moment the pattern stops compiling. The usual culprits are an unbalanced parenthesis or bracket, a dangling quantifier like a lone *, an invalid escape, or a malformed group like (?<). Fix the pattern and the matches reappear instantly — there's no submit button between you and the answer.

What are named groups?

A capture group written as (?<name>…) captures its match under a label instead of just a number, so you can read it as groups.year instead of groups[1]. The tester lists them alongside the numbered groups in every match, clearly marked in blue, which makes complex patterns far easier to reason about.

Why are some of my matches empty?

Anchors (^, $, \b) and lookaround assertions are zero-width — they match a position, not characters, so the matched text is empty. That's correct behavior, and the highlight shows you exactly where they land. The tester also guards against the classic infinite-loop trap where a zero-length match would otherwise run forever.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. Stack has no backend and no database. Matching happens inside your browser tab and the pattern and test string vanish the moment you close or refresh the page. Nothing is logged, because nothing is ever collected.
