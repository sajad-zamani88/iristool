Extract URLs

runs in the bench above

Catches every link that starts with http://, https:// or www. — query strings and fragments included — and strips trailing sentence punctuation so a link followed by a comma comes out clean. Bare domains are deliberately ignored: without a scheme they're too easy to confuse with file names.

Example

Read https://example.com/guide, then visit www.example.com/faq.

↓

https://example.com/guide
www.example.com/faq
2 links — the trailing comma was stripped from the first.

Use it for reference listslink auditssource collectiondead-link prep

Extract Emails

runs in the bench above

Pulls every valid mailbox out of the text: local part, @, domain and a real TLD. Dots, plus signs, hyphens and underscores in the local part are all understood; decoys like "bob (at) example" are ignored because they aren't addresses.

Example

Mail bob@example.com or press@team.example.co.uk — not bob (at) example.

↓

bob@example.com
press@team.example.co.uk
2 mailboxes — the "(at)" decoy is ignored.

Extract Numbers

runs in the bench above

Catches integers, decimals and negatives — and only real numbers. Digits glued to letters ("v2", "room4") are skipped, and digits that belong to a URL or email stay with their parent pattern. What you get is the pure numeric content of the text, in reading order.

Example

Pi is 3.14159, it was -7 degrees, and room v2 holds 100.

↓

3.14159 · -7 · 100 — the "2" in "v2" stays put.

Use it for data scrapingprice listsstatistics gatheringinvoice digits

Extract Hashtags

runs in the bench above

Finds every #tag built from letters, digits and underscores — in any script — while steering clear of lookalikes. HTML entities like &#39; are never mistaken for tags, and a # inside a URL belongs to the URL, not to the hashtag channel.

Example

Follow #launchday and #build_in_public, ignore &#39;entities&#39;.

↓

#launchday · #build_in_public — entities left alone.

Use it for campaign trackingsocial listeningtag researchthread roundups

05 / 05

Extract Mentions

runs in the bench above

Finds every @handle — letters, digits and underscores — and knows the difference between a mention and the @ inside an email address. An @ glued to a word on its left ("bob@") is part of a mailbox, so it's claimed by the email channel instead.

Example

cc @ana_ops, ping @dev_team, mail team@example.com

↓

@ana_ops · @dev_team — the @ in the email stays where it belongs.

Use it for collaborator liststhread summariesattributioncommunity audits

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
Why wasn't "example.com" extracted as a URL?

Only links that start with http://, https:// or www. are matched. Bare domains are skipped on purpose — without a scheme they're ambiguous and easily confused with file names or sentence fragments. Add the protocol and they'll be caught instantly.

Do numbers inside emails or URLs get counted?

No. Overlaps are resolved once, and each span of text belongs to exactly one pattern: URLs beat emails, emails beat mentions and hashtags, and those beat plain numbers. A digit inside an address is part of the address, nothing more — which is exactly what the highlight colors show you.

Can I copy everything from one channel at once?

Yes. Every channel header has a "copy all" button that copies the full list, newline-separated — even when the visible chips are capped on very long texts. And clicking any single chip copies just that find.

Is the text I paste saved anywhere?

No. IrisTool has no backend and no database. All extraction runs inside your browser tab and disappears the moment you close or refresh the page. There is nothing to leak, because nothing is ever collected.

Can I use these tools offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, every extractor on it keeps working offline, because all the logic runs locally in your browser.
