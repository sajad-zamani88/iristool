GPA Calculator

Computes a credit-weighted GPA on the standard 4.0 scale. Enter your grades as letters (A, B+, C−…) or as point values, list the matching credit hours, and it returns the weighted average plus quality points, letter equivalent and honors standing.

Example

Grades A, A−, B+, B, A · credits 3, 4, 3, 2, 3…

↓

GPA 3.65 over 15 credits — cum laude territory.

Use it for semester GPAcumulative GPAhonor roll checksapplication forms

GPA Percentage Converter

Converts in both directions at once: a 4.0-scale GPA becomes a linear percentage (GPA ÷ 4 × 100), and a percentage becomes a GPA (÷ 25). Each direction also shows the matching letter grade, which is what most transcripts and applications actually ask for.

Example

GPA 3.6 · and separately · 85%…

↓

3.6 → 90% (A) · 85% → GPA 3.40 (B+).

Use it for transcript conversioninternational applicationsscholarship formsCV summaries

CGPA Converter

Moves a cumulative grade point between the three scales that appear most often on documents: 10-point CGPA, 4-point GPA and percentage. The percentage uses the widely cited ×9.5 CBSE convention for 10-point CGPAs, plus a plain linear conversion for comparison.

Example

CGPA 8.2 out of 10…

↓

77.9% (×9.5) · 3.28 / 4 · grade band B+.

Use it for university applicationsvisa & job documentsgrade verificationreport cards

Average Calculator

The everyday summary of any list of numbers: paste values separated by commas, spaces or new lines and get the average, sum, count, min, max and range — plus a trimmed average that drops the single highest and lowest values before dividing, which softens the effect of outliers.

Example

12, 15, 18, 21, 24…

↓

Average 18 · sum 90 · range 12.

Use it for test scoresprices & quotesratingsdaily measurements

Mean Calculator

Where the average tool gives you one number, this one compares the four classical means side by side: arithmetic (sum ÷ n), geometric (the product's nth root — right for growth rates), harmonic (right for rates and ratios) and RMS. It adds a 10% trimmed mean and the midrange for context.

Example

2, 4, 8…

↓

AM 4.67 · GM 4.00 · HM 3.43 · RMS 5.29 — ordered exactly as theory predicts.

Use it for growth ratesspeeds & ratessignal magnitudesstatistics homework

Median Calculator

Finds the middle of a data set — the value with half the data below it and half above — and builds the five-number summary around it: median, Q1, Q3, min and max, with the interquartile range that measures the spread of the central 50% without being dragged by extremes.

Example

7, 3, 9, 1, 5 → sorted 1, 3, 5, 7, 9…

↓

Median 5 · Q1 3 · Q3 7 · IQR 4.

Use it for salaries & incomehouse pricesoutlier-proof statsbox plots

Mode Calculator

Finds the value — or values — that appear most often. It reports every mode when a set is bimodal or multimodal, how many times each occurs, what share of the data it covers, and how many distinct values exist in total.

Example

4, 7, 2, 7, 9, 7, 2…

↓

Mode 7 — appears 3× · 42.9% of the set · 4 unique values.

Standard Deviation Calculator

Measures how spread out a data set is around its mean. It computes both the sample standard deviation (n − 1) — the one you want for samples — and the population version (n), together with both variances, the standard error of the mean and the coefficient of variation.

Example

10, 12, 14, 16, 18…

↓

s = 3.16 (sample) · σ = 2.83 (population) · mean 14 · SE 1.41.

Use it for lab reportstest score spreadquality controlresearch data

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
What is the difference between the Average and Mean calculators?

The arithmetic average and the arithmetic mean are the same number — sum divided by count. The Average Calculator focuses on the everyday summary (sum, min, max, range, trimmed average), while the Mean Calculator compares the arithmetic mean with the geometric, harmonic and RMS means, and adds a trimmed mean and the midrange.

Sample vs population standard deviation — which should I use?

Use the sample standard deviation (dividing by n − 1, Bessel's correction) when your data is a sample drawn from a larger population — the common case in surveys, experiments and homework. Use the population standard deviation (dividing by n) only when your data set is the entire population you care about.

Which letter grade scale does the GPA calculator use?

The standard U.S. 4.0 scale: A+/A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, D− = 0.7, F = 0. You can also type numeric point values directly if your school prints them on the transcript.

Is the data I enter saved anywhere?

No. IrisTool has no backend and no database. All calculations run inside your browser tab and disappear the moment you close or refresh the page. Your grades and numbers are never transmitted, stored or logged.

Can I use these calculators offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, every calculator keeps working offline, because all the math runs locally in your browser — there is no server round-trip to fail.
