Epoch Converter

runs in the bench above

Drop in any epoch value and the bench tells you everything it knows about that moment: ISO 8601, the UTC and local renderings, RFC 2822 for mail headers, a plain-English relative reading, and — for the low-level minded — the value in hex and binary. The stat strip decomposes the whole span into days, hours, minutes and seconds since 1970. Quick chips jump to now, an hour or a day ahead, the Moon landing, or the stroke of Y2K.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
What's the difference between Unix time and epoch time?

Nothing — they're two names for the same thing: a count of seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, the Unix epoch. On this page the Unix Timestamp Converter focuses on going back and forth between a timestamp and a calendar date, while the Epoch Converter takes any value and decomposes it into every representation it knows — string formats, relative time, hex and binary.

Seconds or milliseconds — which does the bench expect?

Both, auto-detected. Values whose magnitude is above 100 billion are read as milliseconds — the convention JavaScript and many runtimes use — and anything smaller is read as seconds, the convention of most APIs, databases and operating systems. The readouts always show both, so there's never ambiguity in the output.

Why does my local time differ from UTC?

UTC is the single worldwide reference; your local time is UTC plus your timezone offset, including whatever daylight-saving rule is in force on that particular date — which is why the offset can be different in January than in July for the same place. The bench shows both side by side, and the LOCAL readout names the timezone abbreviation it used.

Can it handle dates before 1970?

Yes — timestamps before the epoch are simply negative, and the bench handles them throughout. Conversion, decomposition, hex and binary all work with negative values, so the Moon landing (around -14159020) converts just as cleanly as tomorrow. The quick chips on the Epoch tab let you verify with one click.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. IrisTool has no backend and no database. Every conversion happens inside your browser tab and disappears the moment you close or refresh the page. There is nothing to leak, because nothing is ever collected.
