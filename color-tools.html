RGB → HSL

runs in the bench above

Re-expresses a color the way designers think about it: hue (the wheel, 0–360°), saturation (the intensity, 0–100%) and lightness (0–100%). HSL is the natural space for generating shades, tints and harmonious palettes — hold hue steady and move lightness, and the color stays in the family.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

( 02 )
Why do RGB and CMYK give different numbers for the same color?

Because they're different physical models. RGB is additive — it describes light emitted by a screen, where red, green and blue add up to white. CMYK is subtractive — it describes ink absorbed by paper, where cyan, magenta, yellow and black subtract from reflected white light. The conversion here is the standard naive transform; professional print uses ICC profiles and will shift values further, so treat the CMYK figures as a solid first estimate.

Does the hex field need the # sign?

No — the bench accepts it with or without the #, in either 3-digit shorthand (#03F) or full 6-digit form (#0033FF), upper or lower case. It normalizes everything to a full uppercase #RRGGBB in the readouts, so what you copy is always the canonical form.

Is converting RGB to HSL lossy?

No — HSL is just a different coordinate system for the same sRGB gamut, so the round trip is exact apart from integer rounding of the hue, saturation and lightness values, which is under one step in each. You can slide H, S and L freely and convert back without ever leaving the space your screen can display.

What CSS does the gradient generator output?

A single background property — linear-gradient(<angle>deg, <stop1>, <stop2>) for linear, or radial-gradient(circle, <stop1>, <stop2>) for radial. It's valid in every modern browser and ready to paste straight into a stylesheet or an inline style attribute.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. IrisTool has no backend and no database. Every conversion happens inside your browser tab and disappears the moment you close or refresh the page. There is nothing to leak, because nothing is ever collected.
